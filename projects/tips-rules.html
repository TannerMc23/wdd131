Disc Golf Tips & Rules

A beginner-friendly crash course to help you throw farther, play smarter, and enjoy the game more.

Backhand Throw Basics

The backhand is the most common and most important throw in disc golf. Here are beginner-friendly fundamentals to help you throw more consistent and farther:

✔️ Start with Stand-Still Throws

Before learning the x-step, beginners should start with stand-still throws to build consistent form.

✔️ Engage Your Lower Body

Power begins from the ground. Shift weight from your back foot to your front foot as you rotate.

✔️ Stay Level

Keep your shoulders level and pull the disc flat across your chest to reduce nose-up throws.

✔️ Pull Straight Across Your Chest

Think "straight line, not curved." This produces cleaner releases and more consistent flights.

✔️ Follow Through

Let your body rotate naturally after release. A full follow-through adds distance and prevents injury.

Forehand Throw Basics

The forehand (or "flick") is great for beginners because it's easier to aim early on.

✔️ Use a two-Finger Grip

Your index and middle finger should press firmly inside the rim while your thumb stays on top.

✔️ Lead With Your Elbow

Begin the motion with the elbow forward-it prevents "wobble" and creates a clean release.

✔️ Wrist Snap Matters Most

Distance comes mostly from wrist snap-not arm strength. Think quick, clean pop.

✔️ Avoid Rolling Your Wrist

Keep your palm facing up at release to avoid unwanted roll-overs and sharp cut-rollers.

Putting Fundamentals

Putting is one of the most important skills in disc golf. Strong putting can save strokes, build confidence, and dramatically improve your overall scores. Beginners should focus on developing a consistent stance and release before worrying about power.

✔️ Find a Consistent Stance

Most beginners start with a straddle or inline (one forward) stance. Neither is "right" - choose whichever feels stable and repeatable. Your feet should give you balance without restricting arm movement.

✔️ Focus on Weight Shift

Good putts use a simple forward weight shift. Start balanced, then let your momentum move slightly toward the basket as you release. Keep it smooth - not a big lunge.

✔️ Aim With Your Center Line

Many putters miss left or right because the body twists during release. Line your chest, arm, and disc along the same vertical plane to throw straight, minimizing wobble.

✔️ Spin vs Push Putting

Spin putting uses wrist rotation and is great for windy conditions. Push putting uses more arm extension with less spin and is very accurate up close. Try both and see which style feels natural.

✔️ Release at the Same Height

A common beginner mistake is changing release points from putt to putt. Choose a height - usually around the chest - and good practice releasing from that consistent level.

✔️ Practice Inside the Circle

Most Putts inside 25 feet are "makeable". Start close (10-15 ft) and build your confidence. Don't immediately practice long putts - repetition in the circle matters the most.

Beginner-Friendly Rules

  • Tee Order: Lowest score from the previous hole throws first.
  • Lie: Where your disc lands, you throw from behind it.
  • Out of Bounds: Add one penalty stroke and throw from the last in-bounds position.
  • Mandatories: Some holes require your disc to pass a designated way, such as left or right of a tree or marker.
  • Putting Circle: Inside 10 meters, you must maintain balance - no falling forward.
  • Fair Play: Don't distract players; wait behind the thrower.
